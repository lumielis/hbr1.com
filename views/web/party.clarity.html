This is the page the port had *redesigned*: a bold yellow year/month strip, a modern seven-column grid with a hover title, and a flat "Coming up" list. Production has none of that. It has three strips of the same link in three colours, a week grid whose cells read `01. Sun`, and a bordered list of events grouped by date. What the legacy template actually emitted, and what is reproduced here: - A `cellSpacing=0 cellPadding=0 width=100%` table with six columns. - The year strip is the first row. It is *not* wrapped in its own row per year: all six years sit in one ``, and the second `` opens with a `colspan=6` spacer cell. - Months and days use ``, i.e. a new row every six cells. Twelve months therefore come out as 6 + 6, and the 30 days of a month as six rows of six with the last row holding the remainder. - Each cell is `` with a link whose `id` is `calendar`, `calendar_found` or `calendar_current`. Those ids are what the legacy inline CSS coloured `#999999` / `#ccee00` / `#0DBD43`, which is why July and August are yellow in production while September is grey. - The day cell text is `CALENDAR_DATE . CALENDAR_DAY`, e.g. `01. Sun`. - Below the grid, a second `cellspacing="1" cellpadding="2"` table holds the events. Each event is: a `#200080` date header (`#cccc66`, `class="head"`) only when the day changes, a 1px `#000066` hairline between events that share a day, a `class="head"` title cut to 30 characters, then a three-column row of city / type / comments and country / flags. The page also has **two SUBMENU bands, each holding two buttons**, and a `(c) HBR1 2001-2003` signature band rather than the `- Music on Futurenet` one the newer pages carry.
